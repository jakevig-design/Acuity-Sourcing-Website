Enterprise software pricing is not transparent. Why Acuity Exists

Twenty years ago I took a job in procurement by accident and ended up spending my career negotiating enterprise software, at GE Vernova, GE Company, BlackRock, the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, Moody's, and Macmillan. Trading platforms, OEM licensing, audit defense, post-M&A consolidation, the works.

Business users found a tool they wanted, started talking to the vendor before anyone had written a real business case, and by the time procurement showed up the deal was already half-built, on the vendor's terms. The requirements were soft. The stakeholders weren't aligned. The budget was either a guess or already disclosed. And the vendor had been running the play since the first demo.

I built Acuity because that pattern is fixable, and almost nobody is fixing it. The work that matters happens before the vendor ever sees a procurement person, and most organizations don't have anyone doing that work. Acuity is a boutique shop focused on exactly that: requirements, business cases, and the internal alignment that makes everything downstream actually work.

Pario

The software I built because my consulting can't scale.

After twenty years of watching the same failure mode show up at every company, I realized the real problem wasn't that buyers were making bad deals. It was that most buyers never had access to someone who'd seen how these deals actually go. By the time a sourcing professional got involved, the damage was done. By the time a consultant like me got involved, the deal was usually too big and the client too sophisticated for the work to scale.

Pario is the software version of the first thing I do on any consulting engagement. You describe the problem. It walks you through a structured scoping conversation, helps you build requirements that hold up commercially, researches the vendor landscape, and generates a business case you can walk into your boss's office with. In thirty minutes you'll have done more rigorous work than most enterprise teams do in a month of internal meetings.

It's not a procurement tool. It's not an RFP platform. It's a preparation tool for the people who will actually have to live with the software they buy, and it's built on the same methodology I use when I'm doing the work myself.
